## Executable boundary

The proposal installs after formal PG73 and review-only v361 admission, v362 grant, v365 custody/start, v366 facts, v367 renewal and v370 no-fetch/renewed-fact functions. Activation is default-off, bound to the direct migrator, exact PG73 version corpus and the actual v370 send-fence trigger table/function identities. Installation rollback leaves no v386 table. The append-only fence stores a request, quote, grant and claim-digest binding, decision digest, nonce and PostgreSQL transaction ID; the trigger takes the existing request advisory lock and refuses a fence while the grant send deadline or any custody/renewal lease is live. These fields record only a future closer's egress boundary. The caller-supplied decision digest is not authenticated evidence.

After a fence commits, table triggers reject new grants, custody, send starts and renewals for that request. They use the stored grant request ID before taking the lock; a supplied row request ID cannot redirect the lock. The v370 no-fetch resolution remains compatible: the fixture resolves a no-start grant first, then adds the v386 fence. A separate no-start case with expired custody adds the v386 fence without a v370 resolution and proves that a direct start INSERT is rejected by **v386's own** trigger. In both cases the grant stays `unknown`, one ordinary and three Guardrail holds remain `dispatched`, counters do not move, and no buyer log appears.

The fixture also holds a real v365 send-start transaction open past its grant deadline while the fence waits on the advisory lock. When the start commits, the fence sees the live custody lease and rejects. It repeats the ordering with a real v367 all-hold epoch-2 renewal held open: the fence waits, then rejects the newly committed live lease. Reverse-order direct inserts of a grant, custody, start or renewal after a committed fence reject. These observed lock waits are local PostgreSQL concurrency evidence, not a fleet or physical stream-stop proof.

**Late evidence stays receivable.** The v386 fence does not block v366/v370 holder fact or independent provider-bill fact insertion. After a real v367 epoch-2 renewal and expiry, the fixture records a v370 epoch-2 `fetch_invoked` observation and a separate v366 `provider_bill` row after the fence. The bill is a synthetic protocol-shaped candidate; the database checks format, role and frozen grant identity, but this test does not establish provider authorship, completeness, billable buyer units or a final cost. A future monetary closer must freeze the fact IDs/digests it cites under the same request lock and send subsequent authenticated bills through an adjustment path. A late holder observation never grants another fetch.

## Exact next seam

A dedicated platform closer must replace the migrator-only fence insertion in the **same transaction** as one immutable attempt resolution, one request terminal, exact ordinary/Guardrail counter transitions, buyer log and a distinct typed platform economic event. It must first obtain independently authenticated final provider evidence and trustworthy buyer units, or retain `unknown` with holds; v366 `provider_bill`, an OpenRouter cost candidate, a holder observation and the v370 no-fetch fence alone cannot authorize zero or actual debit. The v367 hold-mutation trigger, request-scoped buyer counter authority and legacy buyer grant policy require a coordinated cutover. The closer must cite immutable quote/grant/source facts, check account epoch and all holds after locks, fence any live stream, handle COMMIT/close ACK uncertainty and define late-bill adjustments. None of those monetary or runtime gates is implemented by v386.
